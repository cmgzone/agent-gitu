MAKE THE TEAM YOUR OWN

Different strengths.
A shared direction.

These are examples, not fixed job titles. Name your agents and choose the responsibilities that fit your work.

The coordinator

Keeps the goal clear, brings teammate results together, and helps you decide what comes next.

The researcher

Finds relevant information, checks sources, and turns a broad question into a useful brief.

The engineer

Works with the repository, tools, and computer you permit. Makes focused changes and checks the result.

The writer

Turns the shared context into documents, reports, and clear communication you can review.

A CONVERSATION THAT MOVES WORK

Talk to one.
Bring in the team.

Use a direct message for focused work. Start a group when a project needs several perspectives. Mention a teammate to target a request, or let your configured coordinator bring the work together.

  • Separate profiles, models, and skills.
  • Queued messages keep requests in order.
  • Shared artifacts make results easy to inspect.

A LITTLE CONTEXT GOES A LONG WAY

Teammates that fit
the way you work.

Profiles shape the tone and responsibilities. Memory preserves useful preferences. Permissions determine what each agent can actually do.

Personality and roles

Set communication style and responsibilities. Choose “Follow my lead” or “Suggest useful next steps.”

Schedules and follow-ups

Ask for recurring work or a follow-up. Due tasks run while the hosting runtime remains available.

Control at every handoff

Assigned accounts and tool permissions remain scoped to the teammate. Blocking approvals pause dependent work.

Start small. Get useful.

Create one teammate. Give it one clear outcome. Connect only the tools that task needs. Add more roles as your work grows.
